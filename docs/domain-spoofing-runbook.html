Stopping Fraudulent Email Sent as Us

Right now anyone on the internet can send email that appears to come from @outworkemdigital.com, and mailbox providers will deliver it. This is how we shut that down, in the order it has to be done.

What is actually happening

Plain English first

Think about a paper envelope. You write the return address yourself. Nobody at the post office checks whether you really live there. If a stranger writes our address in that corner and drops the letter in a mailbox, it arrives looking like it came from us.

Email works exactly the same way. The "From" line is typed by whoever is sending, and no mail server verifies it on its own. That is not a bug in our website or our hosting. It is how email was designed in the 1980s, and it has never changed.

What has been added since are three notes we publish publicly about our own domain, telling every mail server in the world how to spot a fake. All three exist for us. The problem is that the most important one is currently set to do nothing about what it finds.

The three notes, without the acronyms

SPF
The guest list. It names which mail services are allowed to send on our behalf: Google Workspace, our CRM, our invoicing tool. Ours exists, but it ends with a "soft" instruction, closer to a suggestion than a rule.
DKIM
The wax seal. Every message we send gets stamped with a signature that cannot be forged and breaks if the message is altered in transit. Ours is published and working. Nothing to do here.
DMARC
The standing order. It tells receiving mail servers what to do with a message that fails the guest list and the seal: let it through, send it to spam, or refuse it outright. Ours says "let it through and just tell us about it." This is the whole problem.

Why we cannot just flip the switch

Setting the standing order to "refuse" today would stop the impersonation immediately. It would also stop any of our own legitimate mail that is not correctly signed, which can quietly include invoicing, the CRM, appointment reminders, and newsletters. Those tools often send as us without being on the guest list, and nobody notices until the day they stop arriving.

So the sequence below tightens the rule in stages, watching real delivery data at each step. It takes two to six weeks of mostly waiting, and almost no work.

Part 2 · Stop the form spam

Two form systems, two fixes · can start today

0 of 7 done

A crawl of all 67 public pages found two separate form systems, and they need different fixes. Anti-spam work aimed at one of them cannot touch the other, which is probably why nothing tried so far has helped.

The HighLevel contact form (FvDrMMo4Y5Y85peXUi4K) sits on every single page, it is in the global template. Its submissions never touch our website or our hosting, so no WordPress plugin can see them. It is fixed inside HighLevel.

The Elementor "Blog Optin" form is a real WordPress form, on 40 pages: every blog post, every news post and all six service pages. It posts straight to our own server, and right now it has one email field and no protection of any kind no CAPTCHA, no honeypot, not even a nonce. A script can submit it in a loop. This is the most likely source of the junk.
