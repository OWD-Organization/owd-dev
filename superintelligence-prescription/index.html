The assessment

Five parts of the business, in plain language.

Nothing to prepare. You know the company. We ask about each part and listen for the biggest opening.

Operations

How the day is scheduled, how the office runs, and where work sits and waits.

Fulfillment

How the job gets done in the field, and where quality or time slips.

Sales

How a lead becomes a booked job, and what happens after the estimate.

Marketing operations

How leads are captured, followed up, and reported. The way the marketing work actually runs.

Finance

How money is tracked, billed, and collected.

What we decide

Where technology would matter most.

We are not scoring the company. We are choosing the opportunities that would improve the work, save time, save money, or bring in more revenue.

Quality of work

The job the customer gets, and the work your team has to do over.

Time

Hours the office or the field spends on tasks a tool might carry.

Money

Spend that may not need to be there, or money you could aim more carefully.

Revenue

Work you might be able to book, and are not booking today.

The prescription

You leave with recommendations, not a pile of ideas.

At the end of the hour you have a short list aimed at the opportunities we just found.

Tools

Which tools are worth using for the openings we found in your business.

Workflows

The steps around those tools, so they fit the way your company already works.

Prompts

The wording to give those tools so they do the job you actually need.

Who it is for

Owners who are still close to the work.

HVAC, plumbing, cleaning, landscaping, and similar home service companies. If you know how a job moves from the first call to getting paid, this conversation will make sense.
